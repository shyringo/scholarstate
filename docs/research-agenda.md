# Research Agenda

The next stage should test the protocol against reality rather than add fields speculatively.

## Questions That Can Falsify The Design

1. Can an AI researcher reconstruct why a visible claim has its current status without reading an entire paper bundle?
2. Can two communities apply incompatible evidence rules to the same events without copying or corrupting the underlying record?
3. Can a failed replication be represented without falsely implying that it addresses every scope and condition of the original claim?
4. Can a formal decision remain accountable after its policy, committee membership, or institution changes?
5. Can low-structure material enter the system cheaply and acquire structure incrementally without becoming second-class or being rejected?
6. Can independent implementations merge records deterministically without trusting a central server?
7. Does the cost of verification remain bounded when AI increases claim production by orders of magnitude?

## First Field Trial

Choose one active AI research question with public papers, code, benchmarks, and at least one attempted reproduction. Recruit a small group representing authors, independent replicators, and downstream users. Build the record with AI assistance, then measure:

- time to locate the basis and scope of a claim;
- disagreements caused by extraction errors versus genuine scientific disputes;
- fraction of consequential status changes with a complete responsibility chain;
- effort needed to update the synthesis after new evidence;
- information lost when exporting back to papers, repositories, and existing metadata standards.

The field trial should drive profile design. The universal kernel changes only when the trial exposes a cross-disciplinary invariant or a failure that profiles cannot solve.
