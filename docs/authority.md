# Human Authority Without Global Truth

AI can produce arguments, experiments, reviews, and candidate decisions. It cannot create institutional authority merely by producing more of them. ScholarState separates three layers that document platforms often collapse:

1. **Statement**: what an actor says, with provenance and evidence.
2. **Responsibility**: which identifiable people accept accountability for a consequential act.
3. **Recognition**: which community accepts that act under which policy and jurisdiction.

A formal decision is therefore not a boolean attached to a claim. It is a scoped attestation:

```text
decision = statement + subject + evidence + human responsibility
         + jurisdiction + versioned policy + time
```

The same research object can simultaneously be accepted by one venue, disputed by another community, and unresolved under a third profile. This is not inconsistency in the protocol; it is a faithful record of plural institutions.

## What The Prototype Enforces

- A decisive attestation is rejected when jurisdiction or policy is absent.
- A decisive attestation is rejected when neither its actor nor an anchor identifies a human.
- The projection labels structurally complete decisions `structurally-eligible`, not valid or legitimate.
- AI-authored events remain visible as AI-authored even when a human anchors responsibility.

## What Requires Future Governance

- Identity verification and recovery.
- Delegation from institutions to reviewers, editors, committees, or automated tools.
- Conflicts of interest, recusals, quorum, appeals, and policy migration.
- Privacy-preserving accountability for sensitive review and research.
- Recognition bridges between communities without forcing one global authority.

The protocol should not decide these questions centrally. It must make them expressible, inspectable, and impossible to hide behind an opaque platform score.
